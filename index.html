A note before you begin
Plant symptoms can have many causes. In a real garden, you would consider pests, disease, soil, plant care, and other factors—not just weather. For this activity, focus on the weather or climate-related explanation best supported by the clues provided.

WEIGH THE EVIDENCE

Which explanation best connects the plant response with the conditions shown?

🌎 Climate connection

A single hot spell is weather. As average temperatures rise, very hot days and warm nights can become more common or intense, increasing the chance of conditions that interfere with flowering and fruit set.

WEIGH THE EVIDENCE

Why might this plant wilt during the afternoon?

🌎 Climate connection

One hot afternoon is not evidence of climate change. A warming climate can increase the frequency and intensity of hot conditions, increasing plant heat stress and water demand.

STOP 3 · Pepper

Leaves are drooping and some are beginning to yellow.

Weather snapshot

Daytime high
72–78°F

Nighttime low
52–58°F

Precipitation
3.4 in / 10 days

WEIGH THE EVIDENCE

Which explanation best accounts for the plant symptoms and the weather data?

🌎 Climate connection

One wet period does not establish a climate trend. A warmer atmosphere can hold more moisture, which can contribute to heavier precipitation in many places; how that changes locally depends on region and season.

STOP 4 · Tender growth

New leaves are curled, browned, and darkened in places.

Weather snapshot

Daytime high
58–63°F

Nighttime low
29–31°F

Precipitation
0.25 in / 7 days

WEIGH THE EVIDENCE

Which clues provide the strongest evidence for what may have happened?

🌎 Climate connection

The connection is more complicated than simply “warming means less frost.” Warmer conditions can encourage earlier plant growth, which may leave tender tissue exposed if a later cold event occurs. Frost trends also vary by place and season.
